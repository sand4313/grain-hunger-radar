        How the score works

        Each country gets a risk score from 0 to 100, rebuilt every morning from public data. It measures how exposed a country's food supply is right now. It is an indicator, not a forecast of famine.

        • Hunger, 35%: share of people in IPC Phase 3 (crisis) or worse. 40% scores the maximum. Analyses older than two years are shown but not scored.
        • Currency, 20%: loss against the US dollar over 30 days. A 10% fall scores the maximum.
        • Grain stocks, 25%: wheat, corn and rice ending stocks as a share of a year's use. Below 0.10 scores the maximum, above 0.40 scores zero.
        • Import dependence, 20%: grain imports as a share of use.

        Stocks and imports count for less in richer countries, because a wealthy importer with thin stocks buys more instead of going hungry: high income counts 30%, upper-middle 70%.

        Confidence and caps

        When a part has no data it is left out, and the score rests on the rest. To stop thin data from producing extreme scores:

        • A country can only be Severe (70+) with at least three of the four parts, or an IPC analysis showing 30% or more of people in crisis.
        • A score built on a single non-hunger part is capped below Elevated.
        • Every score carries a confidence label: High (all four parts, or three including hunger), Medium, or Low.

        The tender signal, and how well it has worked

        The tender signal asks one question: is this country likely to import more grain than usual this season? It looks at wheat and corn, for countries that import at least 300,000 tonnes and at least 30% of what they use.

        • High: it bought at least 10% more than its own five-year average last season, and USDA expects this year's harvest to come in below its five-year average.
        • Medium: it bought at least 20% more than its five-year average last season.
        • Low: neither.

        Known limits

        • USDA grain balances for smaller countries are USDA's own estimates, revised each month, not measured figures.
        • IPC hunger analyses exist for about 45 countries and can be several months old. Countries without one are scored on the other three parts.
        • Currency moves use official exchange rates. Where a parallel market rate differs a lot, real stress can be higher than shown.
        • The backtest uses USDA's final, revised figures, so live results may be somewhat weaker than the past hit rates.
        • Daily history, weekly moves and alerts build up over time from the first run on 2 November 2026.
